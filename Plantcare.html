PLANT CARE GUIDE

Everything Your Plant Needs

New to plants? Learn the simple things your plants need to stay healthy, grow well, and look beautiful.
Light

Understand Your Plant's Light Needs

Light is one of the most important things a plant needs. Plants use light to make their food and grow. Before choosing a plant, look at the place where you want to keep it and notice how much natural light it receives.

Remember that bright light does not always mean direct sunlight. Many indoor plants prefer bright light that comes through a window without strong direct sun hitting their leaves.

Low Light

These plants can live in places that receive little natural light. They still need some light, so do not place them in a completely dark room.

Snake Plant · ZZ Plant

Medium Light

These plants usually grow well in bright rooms where they receive indirect sunlight. Keep them near a bright window while protecting them from harsh afternoon sun.

Money Plant · Spider Plant

Bright Light

These plants need plenty of light to grow properly. Succulents and Aloe Vera can handle several hours of direct sunlight when they are gradually introduced to it.

Aloe Vera · Succulents

Simple tip: If your plant is growing slowly, becoming weak, or producing small new leaves, check its light before changing anything else.

Watering

Water Your Plants the Right Way

Water is important, but giving a plant too much water can be more harmful than giving it too little. Many beginners water their plants on a fixed schedule. Instead, check the soil first and water when the plant actually needs it.

Check the Soil

Put your finger into the top few centimeters of soil. If the soil feels dry, it may be time to water. If it still feels wet, wait a little longer.

Water Thoroughly

When the plant needs water, water the soil slowly until some water starts coming out of the drainage holes. This helps the roots receive water properly.

Avoid Overwatering

Do not leave your plant sitting in water for long periods. Constantly wet soil can reduce the air around the roots and may lead to root problems.

Every Plant Is Different

Succulents and cacti usually need less frequent watering, while some tropical plants may need water more often. Temperature, sunlight, pot size, and soil also affect how quickly the soil dries.

Simple tip: Do not water just because it has been several days. Check the soil first.

Soil & Fertilizer

Give Your Plant the Right Soil

Good soil gives plant roots air, water, and support. Different plants need different types of soil, so using the same soil for every plant is not always the best choice.

Potting Soil

A good indoor potting mix works well for many common houseplants. It should be light enough for roots to breathe and should not stay wet for too long.

Cactus & Succulent Mix

Cacti and succulents prefer soil that drains quickly. A special cactus or succulent mix helps prevent excess moisture around their roots.

Organic Compost

Compost adds organic matter and nutrients to soil. Use it as part of a suitable soil mix rather than filling a plant pot with compost alone.

Good Drainage

Healthy soil needs to let extra water move away from the roots. A pot with drainage holes and a suitable soil mix is much safer than soil that stays wet.

Fertilizing

Fertilizer gives plants extra nutrients when they need them for active growth. Follow the instructions on the fertilizer and avoid using more than recommended because too much fertilizer can damage the roots.

Simple tip: Healthy soil is not just about nutrients. Good drainage and enough air around the roots are equally important.

Pot, Drainage & Pruning

Choose the Right Pot & Keep Plants Healthy

A good pot gives your plant enough space for its roots and allows extra water to escape. The right pot does not need to be expensive. It simply needs to suit the plant and its growing conditions.

Choose the Right Pot

Avoid moving a small plant into a very large pot immediately. A pot that is too large can hold more wet soil around the roots for longer. Choose a pot that gives the roots enough room to grow.

Drainage Holes Matter

Always look for drainage holes when choosing a pot for most houseplants. They allow extra water to escape and help reduce the chance of waterlogged soil.

Repotting

A plant may need a larger pot when its roots become crowded, growth slows, or the soil dries unusually quickly. Repotting is not something you need to do every few months without a reason.

Pruning

Pruning means removing dead, damaged, or unwanted parts of a plant. It can help keep the plant neat and encourage healthy new growth. Use clean and sharp scissors or pruning tools.

Remove Damaged Leaves

Remove completely dead or badly damaged leaves so the plant can focus its energy on healthy growth. Do not remove healthy leaves without a reason.

Simple tip: Before repotting or pruning, first understand why the plant needs it. Avoid changing everything at once when a plant is struggling.
